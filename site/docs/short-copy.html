Written 28 August 2026. Two blurbs at roughly 300 characters, for cold outreach, a job post, an intro email, or anywhere the pitch has to fit in a paragraph. Sourced from Projector-Product-Spec.md and Projector-Investor-Deck.md.

What it is (291 characters)

Projector is a curator-led film companion. A curator picks a film, sets a showtime, and hosts the room. You watch on your own TV, on whatever service you already pay for, while their notes arrive in sync on a second screen alongside live chat. Pop-Up Video, live, with a film programmer.

The no-player, no-rights architecture is carried implicitly by "whatever service you already pay for," which is the part that makes the model work. Say it explicitly if the reader is technical or an investor.

What the engineer is for (297 characters)

I'm building the screens myself, with coding agents, in a repo. I need an engineer for the spine: a server-authoritative session clock that stays honest across a hundred phones for two hours, the note-firing engine on top of it, chat and presence, ticketing, and the row-level security underneath.

Leaves out intermission, failure states, the twelve days across ten weeks, and the explicit "do not build Stripe Connect." Those are in Projector-Engineer-Brief.md for anyone who asks a second question.

An alternative angle

Both blurbs above lead with the viewing experience. The deck's problem slide argues the wedge is the curator's economics rather than the viewer's boredom: a film programmer with 800,000 followers has a job at a theatre and a Letterboxd account, and no way to turn that into a live event with an income. Lead there instead when the reader is an investor or a curator, and lead with the viewing experience when they are neither.
